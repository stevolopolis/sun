**Important information**:

- **Total Observation Period:** There are **19,232 calendar days** in total across the observation window, spanning **January 1, 1973 to August 27, 2025** inclusive.
- **Station Record Completeness:**
  - **19,232 days (100% complete):** Des Moines, San Francisco, St. Louis, Seattle, and Atlanta have zero missing days.
  - **19,147–19,230 days (>99.5% complete):** Caribou (19,230), New Orleans (19,229), Tucson (19,228), Arcata (19,220), Christchurch (19,169), and Reykjavík (19,147).
  - **13,349 days (~69.4% complete):** Addis Ababa (Bole) has 13,349 valid observation days due to historical station reporting gaps.

### What the visualization shows

Across all six pairs, extreme temperature frequency is driven by maritime moderation, continentality, elevation, and moisture:

- **Maritime vs. Continental Asymmetry (Pairs 1, 2, and 5):** Coastal maritime stations (Arcata, San Francisco, Reykjavík) rarely exceed 90 °F (0.03%–1.4% of days) and experience few freezing nights. Their paired continental counterparts (Des Moines, St. Louis, Caribou) experience frequent extremes in both directions, with Caribou enduring freezing nights on nearly 49% of all days (9,402 nights) and St. Louis logging 2,648 days over 90 °F (13.8%)
- **Elevation and Latitude Buffers (Pairs 3 and 4):** Equatorial highland Addis Ababa enjoys year-round thermal stability with almost no extreme heat (0.06%) and zero freezing nights, contrasting with subtropical Atlanta's balanced extremes (~11%–12% for both hot days and freezing nights). Meanwhile, Seattle and Christchurch, which are mid-latitude and oceanic, exhibit comparable, mild extremes with low freezing frequencies (7.5% vs. 10.6%)
- **Arid Heat Multiplication (Pair 6):** Arid Tucson records extreme heat on over 43% of days (8,289 days >90 °F) due to dry air and intense desert insolation, more than doubling humid subtropical New Orleans (21.1%), while both maintain low freezing night counts (~2%–3%)
