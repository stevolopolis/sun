### Visualization

The solid lines are a centered moving average of each station's daily mean temperature. Dotted lines mark each station's average over the span shown. The gray lines sit 5 °F below and above the pair's long-term average. A shaded band marks each time a curve crosses from one gray line to the other, and its label counts the days from the last day at one line to the first day at the other. The figure opens on San Francisco and St. Louis in 1985, the year whose crossings come closest to both stations' 1973–2024 medians.

### What the visualization shows

San Francisco and St. Louis differ by only **0.16 °F** in long-term mean temperature (57.07 and 57.23 °F), but they move between seasons at very different speeds. In 1985, St. Louis's 7-day average climbed from 52.2 °F to 62.2 °F in **5 days** (April 10 to 15). San Francisco's took **70 days** (March 28 to June 6). In autumn, St. Louis fell back across both lines in **7 days**, and San Francisco in **33 days**.

1985 is typical. Over 1973–2024, the median spring crossing of the 7-day average took **79 days** in San Francisco and **5 days** in St. Louis. The median autumn crossing took **44 days** and **7 days**. San Francisco warms more slowly than it cools, while St. Louis's two transitions take about the same time.

The month window smooths out short warm and cold spells, but the gap remains: the median spring crossing takes about **148 days** in San Francisco and **26 days** in St. Louis. Arcata and Des Moines show the same contrast. With the week window, their median spring crossings take **77 days** and **6 days**.
